Back-of-House Staffing Planner

Which Crown Melbourne outlets get busy, at what time, and where the kitchen and stewarding roster falls short, so shifts can be rostered to match demand instead of the same pattern every day.

Sample data. Outlet names are real Crown venues, but covers, staffing standards and the roster are modelled for demonstration. Swap in real POS covers and roster exports to use it for real (see "Use it with real data" at the bottom).

What this shows, in plain English

    1. When is each outlet busy?

    How busy each outlet is, hour by hour, compared with its own busiest hour of the week. Brighter = busier. This is what the roster should follow.

    How to read: each row is an outlet, each column an hour. Hover a square for the exact numbers.

    2. Where are we short-staffed?

    Kitchen and stewarding staff needed (from covers) minus staff rostered, for every outlet and hour.

    How to read: red squares need more staff (the number is how many), blue squares have spare staff, grey is right.

    Short-staffed About right Spare staff

    Across all outlets: staff needed vs rostered

    Gold = staff the covers call for. Blue = staff on the roster. Where gold is above blue, kitchens run short.

    Every day of the week

    Staff-hours short (red) and spare (blue). The same roster every day leaves weekends short and early week heavy.

    3. Roster changes to make

    The biggest gaps for the selected day. First use staff who are spare in the same kitchen earlier in the day (start them later), then spare staff in other outlets (kitchen hands and stewards move more easily than chefs), and only then add a short shift.

    Use it with real data

    1. Covers by hour: export from the outlet POS (or reservations system) as outlet, day, hour, covers.
    2. Roster by hour: export the BOH roster (chefs, kitchen hands, stewards) as outlet, day, hour, staff rostered.
    3. Staffing standard: agree with each head chef how many covers one BOH staff member can handle per hour (here: 10 for fine dining, 20 to 30 for casual and buffet, 40 for banquets).
    4. Events: add expected covers for event days (Australian Open, AFL Grand Final, Melbourne Cup, big banquets) from the events calendar.
    5. Save the two files into data/ with the same columns and re-run python src/build_dashboard.py.
    How the numbers are worked out

    Staff needed in an hour = covers ÷ the outlet's staffing standard, rounded up, with at least 1 person whenever the outlet is open. Gap = staff needed − staff rostered. Staff-hours short = the sum of all positive gaps. The sample roster uses the same 4-hour shift blocks every day, sized for the busiest hour of an average day, which is how a flat roster behaves. Busyness in chart 1 = covers ÷ the outlet's busiest hour across the week (including event days).
